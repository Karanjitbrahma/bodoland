From Kokrajhar Weaves to Global Exports: How Aaroki Silks Scaled to ₹2.4 Cr Annual Revenue

Founded by 26-year-old Anusuiya Brahma, the sustainable handloom enterprise connects over 140 home-weavers to ethical fashion retail across metropolitan hubs and European boutiques.

Core Thesis: By decentralizing preliminary warp preparations to indigenous rural households and standardizing certified natural madder and indigo dyeing in centralized micro-hubs, Aaroki Silks removed 4 tiers of Guwahati-Kolkata intermediary cartels.

The Middleman Dilemma: Trapped in Wholesale Mandis

In the dense bamboo-fringed compounds of Titaguri and Dotoma blocks, handloom weaving has never been merely an occupation—it is an inherited architectural language. Nearly every Bodo household hosts an indigenous fly-shuttle or traditional throw-shuttle wooden loom. For generations, women spent hundreds of hours tending castor-fed silkworms to extract cruelty-free Eri silk, only to encounter severe exploitation at regional commodity auctions.

"When we began in early 2022, a weaver invested 42 hours across two weeks to complete a single standard Eri stole," recounts founder Anusuiya Brahma. "Guwahati aggregators bought the finished fabric for ₹1,200 to ₹1,400 per piece. By the time that same stole surfaced in South Delhi lifestyle boutiques or Milanese showrooms, it retailed upwards of ₹14,500. The artisan received barely 9% of retail value."

format_quote
"We were producing some of the world's finest wild silk, but the economic value was completely captured in mainland showrooms, not in the villages where it was painstakingly spun."
Anusuiya Brahma • Founder, Aaroki Silks

Building the Distributed Production Cluster

Rather than establishing a centralized factory system that disrupted domestic agrarian obligations, the enterprise formulated a decentralized hub-and-spoke production infrastructure across two aggregation checkpoints in Dotoma and Kokrajhar town, equipped with precision digital moisture-testers and electronic weighing balances.

Process Innovation

Zero-Effluent Botanical Dye Formulation

Substituting synthetic aniline chemicals with wild madder (Manjistha), indigenous tea leaves, and wild lac resin, yielding an eco-certification compliant with European REACH standards.

Key strategic playbooks distilled from this enterprise's scale trajectory across the Bodoland Territorial Region:

1

Traceability as a Moat

Every exported unit carries an authentication QR documenting the artisan profile and village coordinates, justifying pricing premiums overseas.

2

Standardize Packaging Locally

Avoid shipping unfinished raw goods to mainland aggregation hubs. Local high-grade finishing and eco-packaging retains maximum domestic value.

3

Bypass Consignment Traps

Refuse retail partnerships demanding 90-120 day consignment cycles. Enforce advance deposits to protect grassroots artisan working liquidity.

4

Build for Micro-Settlements

Integrate automated bi-weekly UPI direct settlements to producers' bank accounts. Rapid friction-free liquidity triggers unprecedented loyalty.
